# Who We Sell To — v2 (boss-set 2026-07-30)

Replaces the original "any local business with no website" targeting.

---

## Why we changed

512 Studio — our warmest lead, saw a finished site with their own photos —
said **"too expensive"** at $300. Barbers and salons across the board have
been stingy or silent. That's not a pitch failure. It's math:

| Business | Revenue per customer | What $300 feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Barber | ~$25/cut | 12 haircuts. Huge. |
| Salon | ~$40-120 | Several full days of one chair. |
| Remodeler | $15,000-40,000/job | Rounding error. One job pays it 50x. |
| Roofer | $8,000-25,000/job | Less than a single square of shingles. |

**A website only sells to someone whose customer is worth enough to justify
chasing one more of them.** Beauty businesses fail that test. High-ticket
trades pass it easily.

## The new rule (boss's words)

> "People who have the money and clients, and want MORE work — that's where
> we step in."

Three filters, all must be true:
1. **High ticket** — one job is worth $3,000+
2. **Already busy** — they have clients, they're not desperate. Proof they're
   good at the work and have cash flow.
3. **Growth appetite** — actively want more jobs, not coasting to retirement

## TARGET (go here)

**High-ticket trades & contractors**
- Remodeling / kitchen &amp; bath
- Custom home builders
- Concrete &amp; excavation / dirt work
- Roofing &amp; siding
- Decks, fences, hardscape / outdoor living
- Custom fabrication, welding, machine shops
- Septic, well drilling, land clearing

**High-ticket specialty auto / marine**
- Restoration shops, performance / diesel shops
- Boat &amp; powersports dealers, marine service
- High-end detailing with ceramic (MG, MN Detail already fit here)

**High-ticket services**
- Wedding &amp; event venues, photographers, planners
- Commercial cleaning / janitorial (B2B contracts, recurring)
- Equipment rental, trailer sales

## DO NOT TARGET (learned the hard way)

- **Barbers, salons, nail, lash, beauty** — boss-set, 2026-07-30. Low ticket,
  price sensitive, said no repeatedly. Existing built mockups stay live but
  no new outreach here.
- Food trucks / small restaurants — thin margins
- Anyone whose average sale is under ~$500

## PRICING — must change with the market

The old $300 flat was **too expensive for barbers AND too cheap to be
credible for contractors.** A guy running $30k jobs sees $300 and assumes
amateur work.

**New structure:**

| Tier | Price | For |
|---|---|---|
| Starter site | **$800** | 1-page, small trade, quick close |
| Standard site | **$1,500** | Multi-page, gallery, quote form — the default |
| Care plan | **$50-100/mo** | Hosting, edits, updates — recurring, the real prize |

**Why this works:** at $1,500, one $20,000 remodel job pays for it 13x. The
ROI argument does the selling. And 3-4 care plans = the $200/month goal,
permanently.

## THE NEW PITCH ANGLE

Stop selling "a website." Start selling **more jobs.**

Old: "You don't have a website, so people can't find you."
New: "You're getting referrals, but when someone gets your name and googles
you, there's nothing there. That's a job going to the guy who shows up."

The opener stays short (SHORT-DMS rules still apply — one question, under 25
words), but the value conversation on msg 2-3 is about **revenue, not web
presence.**

## What still applies

- Verify no site (or a bad/outdated one) before pitching — house rule 6
- Verify provably active — house rule 9
- Short human DMs — house rule 3
- Build them a real mockup before/right after they reply. That part worked.

## GEOGRAPHIC LAYER (added 2026-08-08)

Stacking three filters multiplies the odds. Each one alone is weak; together
they point at the least-likely-to-have-a-website business in America.

### Filter 1 — Niche (biggest lever)
| Segment | No website |
|---|---|
| US small business average | **27%** |
| **Certain trades** | **up to 60%** |

Trades more than double the base rate. This matters more than any state.

### Filter 2 — State (low AI / low digital adoption)
Census BTOS 2026 AI adoption by state:

| Lowest | Rate |
|---|---|
| **West Virginia** | **10.8%** (lowest in US) |
| Mississippi, Arkansas, Kentucky, Alabama, Louisiana | low cluster |

For contrast, highest: Colorado 23.2%, Arizona 22.9%, DC 22.5%.

Low adoption = fewer competitors pitching them, and less likely to have
already bought a site.

### Filter 3 — Small towns, not the metro
Skip Charleston, Jackson, Little Rock, Louisville. Target towns of
5k-40k people. Metro contractors have marketing budgets and agencies.

### The intersection
**A concrete / roofing / excavation contractor in a small West Virginia,
Mississippi, Arkansas or Kentucky town** is statistically the best target
we can define.

### Honest counterweights
- Low AI adoption cuts both ways: less competition, but also lower tech
  budgets and slower decisions. Same wall 512 hit, different state.
- Out of state means losing "I'm local," which is a real trust cost with
  contractors who buy on relationships. Not fatal (Kz's is WI, Christ
  Centered is OK), but it is a cost.
